\section{Related Work}
EuroSAT provides labeled Sentinel-2 image patches for land-use and land-cover classification and establishes deep-learning baselines for this task~\cite{helber2019}. Our study uses its RGB representation; it does not assess the contribution of the remaining spectral bands. Residual networks provide the underlying image-classification architecture~\cite{he2016}.

Label distributions can differ substantially between distributed data owners. NIID-Bench studies data-partition strategies and learning behavior under heterogeneous data silos~\cite{li2022}. We adapt its label-Dirichlet partitioner to the fixed EuroSAT training subset, retaining the balancing mask and minimum-client-size condition. A synthetic label partition represents one controlled form of heterogeneity, rather than a geographic partition.

Two established objectives motivate the loss comparison. Cui et al. weight classes through the effective number of training examples~\cite{cui2019}. Lin et al. introduce focal loss to reduce the relative contribution of confidently classified examples~\cite{lin2017}. Our implementation compares effective-number weighted softmax cross-entropy with multiclass softmax focal loss and ordinary cross-entropy. Improvements on other datasets or on detection do not establish effectiveness for the present setting.

Remote-sensing federated-learning research compares algorithms on decentralized archives, including multi-label classification and different heterogeneity conditions~\cite{buyuktas2023}. In contrast to that comparative FL study, the present protocol holds a lightweight single-label classifier and global evaluation split fixed while comparing loss functions under two synthetic training distributions. Client models are trained independently; no server aggregation or communication algorithm is evaluated. This is a controlled empirical scope, not a claim of a new federated-learning method or an unqualified first study.
